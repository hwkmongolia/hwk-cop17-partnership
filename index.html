WHY MONGOLIA • WHY HWK

Mongolia Is Our Living Classroom

Forests, steppe landscapes, rivers, wetlands, wildlife habitats, migratory bird routes and nomadic heritage create extraordinary opportunities to learn directly from place.

HWK brings people into these environments to explore the connections between biodiversity, water, land and human knowledge through guided field learning.

Nature becomes the classroom.

Field observation is combined with knowledge from researchers, conservation practitioners, educators, rangers and local knowledge holders.

We do not simply visit nature. We use direct experience to make environmental relationships visible and understandable.

REAL FIELD LEARNING

Knowledge Begins With Seeing for Yourself

HWK field experiences bring participants closer to species, ecosystems and the people who understand them. A bird, an insect, a wetland or a river can become the starting point for understanding a much larger environmental system.

Bogd Khan Mountain • Biodiversity
BOGD KHAN MOUNTAIN • BIODIVERSITY

Learning to Read a Forest Through Birds

Bird observation opens a window into the wider forest ecosystem. Participants learn to use binoculars, spotting scopes and field guides while exploring how birds live within their habitats.

With expert guidance, observation becomes more than identification — it becomes a way to understand biodiversity, ecological relationships and why habitats matter.

HWK Evidence: HWK’s existing forest bird programme combines field observation, bird identification, equipment skills and forest-ecosystem learning at Bogd Khan Mountain.
Field observation → scientific curiosity → biodiversity awareness
Khun Lake • Tuul River • Wetland Learning
KHUN LAKE • TUUL RIVER • WETLAND LEARNING

Follow the Birds. Discover the Wetland.

A wetland is more than water. At Khun Lake and along the Tuul River, participants can explore waterbirds, aquatic life and the habitat relationships that connect water, land and biodiversity.

Observation with binoculars and field guides can be combined with learning about wetland ecosystems and aquatic invertebrates.

HWK Evidence: HWK’s waterbird field activities connect Khun Lake, Songino Bay and the Tuul River with wetland learning, waterbird observation and basic aquatic-invertebrate learning.
See the habitat → understand the connections → care about the system
Water • Wetlands • Ecosystem Connections
WATER • WETLANDS • ECOSYSTEM CONNECTIONS

One Species Can Reveal an Entire River System

Beaver ecology provides a practical way to explore how water, vegetation, wetlands and biodiversity interact.

By learning how beavers build dams and channels and alter local habitat conditions, participants can begin to see a river not as an isolated waterway, but as a connected ecological system.

HWK Evidence: HWK uses Eurasian beaver ecology, the Tuul River ecosystem, water pollution and freshwater biodiversity as field-learning themes.
Species → habitat → water → biodiversity → stewardship
Insects • Macro Photography • Biodiversity
INSECTS • MACRO PHOTOGRAPHY • BIODIVERSITY

See Closer. Learn Deeper.

Some of the most powerful biodiversity lessons begin with organisms small enough to overlook.

Macro photography encourages participants to slow down, observe structure and behaviour, compare what they see and turn close observation into questions about adaptation, pollination, food webs and ecosystem roles.

Learning Sequence: Observe → Photograph → Identify → Share
When biodiversity becomes visible, it becomes easier to understand and value.

KNOWLEDGE IN THE FIELD

Learning With People Who Know the Landscape

HWK brings practical knowledge into the field by connecting participants with researchers, conservation practitioners, educators, rangers and local knowledge holders.

Their role is not simply to give a lecture. They help participants observe more carefully, ask better questions and connect what they see with wider environmental systems.

PARTNERSHIP OPPORTUNITIES

Three Ways to Build Field Learning Together

HWK is developing three practical opportunities for organizations interested in youth, biodiversity, water, land and traditional knowledge. Each can be co-created with partners through funding, expertise, research, education and institutional collaboration.

OPPORTUNITY 01
Youth & Biodiversity

Biodiversity in the Field

A youth field-learning programme that turns direct observation of biodiversity into scientific learning, curiosity and responsibility toward nature.

Explore through:

Birds • Insects & Pollinators • Forest Plants • Wildlife • Wetland Biodiversity

Partnership Relevance:

Partners can support programme development, scientific expertise, field equipment, educational resources, research collaboration and appropriate citizen-science methodology.

OPPORTUNITY 02
Water & Ecosystem

Connected Waters

A field-based programme connecting freshwater ecosystems, wetlands, biodiversity and water stewardship through practical environmental learning.

Explore through:

Rivers • Wetlands • Aquatic Life • Waterbirds • Beaver Ecology • Water Stewardship

Partners can contribute freshwater and wetland expertise, biodiversity research, technical capacity, equipment, educational resources, research or monitoring collaboration and programme support.

OPPORTUNITY 03
Rangeland, Nomadic Knowledge & Youth

Living Rangelands

An intergenerational field-learning programme connecting young people with rangeland stewardship, seasonal pasture knowledge and Mongolia’s living nomadic knowledge.

Seasonal Movement • Pasture Knowledge • Land & Livestock • Herder Knowledge • Youth Learning
